Litti Chokha: The Soul of Bihari Cuisine

Description & History

Litti Chokha is a rustic, earthy, and immensely flavorful traditional dish that forms the very heart of Bihari cuisine. It consists of two main components: Litti, which are round, baked dough balls made of whole wheat flour and stuffed with a spiced mixture of sattu (roasted black gram/chickpea flour), and Chokha, a smoky, savory mash of fire-roasted eggplants, tomatoes, and boiled potatoes.

Historically, Litti Chokha originated as a practical and nutritious survival food for peasants, farmers, and travelers in the Magadh region. Because it requires very little water to prepare and can last for days without spoiling, it was the perfect travel companion. It is even said that the dish played a crucial role during the 1857 Sepoy Mutiny, serving as the primary ration for the rebel armies because it could be baked quickly over makeshift campfires without the need for utensils.

Today, whether it is being enjoyed in the historic, culturally rich streets of Darbhanga or served fresh at the massive, vibrant cattle fairs of Sonpur, Litti Chokha is a celebrated comfort food. The traditional method of roasting the Litti over cow dung cakes or wood charcoal gives it a distinct smoky flavor, which is then perfectly balanced by generously dipping the hot, cracked Litti into pure, melted ghee (clarified butter).

Now we wil understand the ingredients and recipie of the litti-chokha

ingredients-list:

for litti Dough:

  • 2 cups Whole Wheat Flour (Atta)
  • 1/2 teaspoon Carom Seeds (Ajwain)
  • 2 tablespoons Ghee (Clarified Butter)
  • 1/2 teaspoon Salt
  • Water (as needed to knead a semi-stiff dough)

for sattu stuffing:

  • Water (as needed to knead a semi-stiff dough)
  • 1 medium Onion (finely chopped)
  • 4-5 cloves of Garlic (minced)
  • 1-inch piece of Ginger (grated)
  • 2-3 Green Chilies (finely chopped)
  • 2 tablespoons Fresh Coriander Leaves (chopped)
  • 1 tablespoon Mustard Oil (raw, for authentic pungency)
  • 1 tablespoon Mango Pickle Masala (Achar Ka Masala - adds tanginess)
  • 1 tablespoon Lemon Juice
  • Salt to taste

    • For the Chokha:

    • 1 large Eggplant (Baingan/Aubergine)
    • 3 medium Tomatoes
    • 2 medium Potatoes (boiled and peeled)
    • 4-5 cloves of Garlic (peeled)
    • 2 Green Chilies (chopped)
    • 1-inch piece of Ginger (finely chopped)
    • 1 tablespoon Raw Mustard Oil
    • 2 tablespoons Fresh Coriander Leaves (chopped)
    • Salt to taste

    Now let's learn how to prepare delicious litti-chokha:

      steps for making chokha:

    1. Roast the Vegetables: Make slight slits in the eggplant and tuck the garlic cloves inside. Roast the eggplant and tomatoes directly over an open gas flame until the skin is completely charred and the insides are soft and mushy.
    2. Peel and Mash: Once cooled, peel the blackened skin off the eggplant and tomatoes. Place them in a large bowl along with the boiled potatoes.
    3. Mix the Chokha: Mash the roasted eggplant, tomatoes, and potatoes together. Add the chopped ginger, green chilies, coriander leaves, raw mustard oil, and salt. Mix everything thoroughly. Set aside.

      Now we will learn how to make litti:

      1. Knead the Dough: In a mixing bowl, combine the whole wheat flour, carom seeds, salt, and ghee. Gradually add water and knead it into a firm, semi-stiff dough. Cover with a damp cloth and let it rest for 20 minutes.
      2. Prepare the Stuffing: In a separate bowl, mix the sattu with the chopped onions, garlic, ginger, green chilies, coriander, mustard oil, pickle masala, lemon juice, and salt. Sprinkle a tiny splash of water so the mixture binds together slightly but remains crumbly.
      3. Stuff the Dough: Divide the rested dough into equal-sized lemon balls. Flatten each ball into a small cup shape using your thumbs. Place 1-2 tablespoons of the sattu filling into the center, gather the edges, seal tightly, and roll it back into a smooth, round ball.
      4. Bake the litti:
        • Traditional Method: Roast the balls slowly over a charcoal grill until they are golden brown, cooked on the inside, and slightly cracked open.
        • Oven Method: Preheat the oven to 200°C (390°F) and bake the littis for 30-40 minutes, flipping them halfway, until the crust is golden and firm.
      5. serve: The most crucial step! Break open the hot Litti slightly from the top, dunk it entirely into a bowl of melted ghee, and serve immediately alongside the smoky Chokha, a side of sliced onions, and green chutney.

    NOw, the recipie for litti-chokha is done. Enjoy with your family.
